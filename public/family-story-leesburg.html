Two generations of early mornings

Why “Grampy’s”

“The name ‘Grampy’ has a double meaning. My grandchildren already call me Grampy, so we wanted to use something that had meaning to our family — it’s where my work ethic started.” — Shawnn Lynch, founder, to the Daily Commercial.

The bakers

Three names on the operating license

SL

Owner · founder

Shawnn Lynch

A Leesburg-area kitchen-and-bath contractor who has been baking since he was old enough to stand on a box in the kitchen. He learned the work ethic from his grandfather — the Grampy the bakery is named for.

LH

Co-owner

Lorie Howell

Grampy’s operating partner. “We will do a soft opening, giving people a sample of the bakery items and take their input on what we can do better,” she told the paper about the first days.

HL

Co-owner

Heather Lynch

The third co-owner on the license. “We are excited about opening and being able to share our baked goods with the community,” she said when the county issued the certificate of occupancy.

The road to Nov 24

A year of delays, kept anyway

  1. September 2025Grampy’s Bakery files its operating license with the county; the team takes over a leased storefront on Griffin Road that retail had struggled to hold for more than fifteen years.
  2. Winter 2025–26The estimate for required repairs runs $45,000. Lynch — a contractor by trade — signs for the space anyway and does the repairs and upgrades with his own hands.
  3. Late February 2026With mid-February opening slipped, the last hired contractor pulls out days before the hood goes in. The opening moves again — to about Easter.
  4. 2026, spring–summerA new subcontractor installs the hood; the buildout grows into a production bakery — second convection oven, two refrigerators, three industrial mixers. Plan review and the building inspection follow.
  5. November 2026The county issues the certificate of occupancy. The grand opening lands: Tuesday, November 24, 11:45 a.m., ribbon-cutting with the Leesburg Area Chamber of Commerce.
It’s a go at the Griffin Road location.
Shawnn Lynch, founder — on the certificate of occupancy

The plan now

World-class, on purpose

What started as one shop’s worth of holiday baking grew, delay by delay, into a bigger plan: a world-class commercial bakery operation, with seasonal menus that change as the year turns. The soft opening starts by asking customers what they want to see in the cases.
